The applicant's shadow diagramsEllivo Architects, DA-600 and DA-601, revision 8, dated 14/05/2026, lodged with the development application for 12 Chalk Street and obtained from Council's development application record. Reproduced here for comparison in the appeal. Both sheets are raster scans with no vector geometry, so each of the eleven panels was registered to this model's frame by image correlation against the approved footprints, which are known exactly. The fit resolved to grid north — rotation 0.00° — and 0.1700 m per pixel, against the 0.1693 m implied by the sheets' own “1:1000 @ A1” printed on A3: two independent routes agreeing to 0.4%. Each panel is warped into the same frame as the ground base and drapes with the identical transform. Their shadow fill is recoloured teal so it can be told apart from the shade this model casts; nothing else about the panels is altered. Tick as lodged beside the panel chooser to drape the sheet with its own greys untouched instead, so nothing here is only ever shown recoloured. Note that their diagrams shade every building on the plan, while the shade drawn here is cast by the approved buildings alone, so the two extents are not like for like.
The eight-storey comparatorThe 2020 approval fixed height as a storey count only — no top RL appears in any source. R1-079 therefore tests a bounded family at RL 46.0, 48.0, 50.0, 52.0 and 55.75; RL 48.0 is the true eight-storey plate. Across that whole family the peak incremental loss to Cluster 5 is identical to 0.001 m², because no comparator in the range reaches those backyards at the governing instants. This is a bounded comparator, not the applicant's model, and must never be described as one.
Cluster 4 statusThe eight Type F dwellings and their gardens are derived, not traced: POS bands from R1-072 Rev C, footprints reconstructed from the stamped unit-sheet ground extent (4199 × 7650 mm) clipped to the DCDB lot — they reproduce the sheet area to ±0.4 m². Their floor-to-floor is not source-locked, so the level lines are indicative. Their gardens sit south of their own houses, so figures here subtract shading by the dwellings themselves; without that subtraction Cluster 4 is badly overstated.
Earlier Greville stagesThe terrace continuing west of Cluster 4 is drawn too — 75 m long, ~16 units, at 9.5 m above natural ground per MOD_Pt1 sheets p8/p10/p12 (Clusters 1–3, Types D and E). No DCDB was pulled for those lots, so its outline comes from the registered orthophoto and it is drawn as one attached terrace rather than unit by unit; its ground level is assumed. It sits down-sun of the receptors at the governing instants and changes none of them.
Heritage laundryMOD_Pt1 TP51.01 labels it but no held sheet carries its outline as vector geometry or any height at all. The plan outline is segmented from the registered orthophoto and simplified to a main range plus its western wing — 879 m², 65 m long — and the ridge is assumed at 7.5 m. Its long axis fits at bearing 3.65°, not the estate's 8.86°: it predates the subdivision and sits on its own alignment. That assumption is bounded, not guessed at: every ridge from 6 m to 12 m gives an identical result at every receptor, and at each Cluster 5 governing instant it casts 0.000 m² onto those backyards regardless — those four instants are unchanged to the digit by adding it. It does reach them at very low sun, so it is modelled as a caster; where it intercepts, the figure here falls below the frozen peak, which the frozen model did not credit. That is a reduction, never an increase.
