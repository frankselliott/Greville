How tall would it have to be?

The frozen model asks what the approved buildings take from these backyards. This page asks the question backwards: at a given moment, what is the lowest an envelope could be and still reach this ground at all? That height is the first-contact RL. Where it sits above the bounded comparator family, no building in that family reaches — and the year map shows every hour of 2026 at once.

This page computes the inverse; it does not restate the frozen results. The forward figures — losses, peaks, otherwise-sunlit areas — are finalised and live in the frozen visualiser. Nothing here alters them. The inverse is also a known open item: it is reproduced from the frozen browser method, which disagrees with the frozen offline first_contact_RL table by up to 0.32 m. The bounded comparator family

The site What is casting, and where the requirement comes from

What is drawnThe comparator is a bounded family of prisms at RL 46.0, 48.0, 50.0, 52.0 and 55.75, tested because the 2020 approval fixed height as a storey count only — no top RL appears in any source. RL 48.0 is the true eight-storey plate. This is a bounded comparator, never the applicant's model, and must not be described as one.
MethodNOAA geometric solar position, UTC+10, no daylight saving, no refraction — the frozen convention. Geometry in EPSG:7856 minus the local origin. For each sample point the ray toward the sun is clipped against each comparator ring by half-plane intersection; the rings are convex, so the clip is exact with no sampling along the ray. The entry distance gives the height a prism must reach to cast that far, and the smallest such height over the receptor is the first-contact RL. Ground is drawn flat; the real site slopes.
The two methods, and why they differThis reproduces the frozen browser method, which takes a minimum over grid sample points. The frozen offline first_contact_RL table was produced another way: 16 of its 21 values differ by more than 10 mm, worst 0.32 m, and refining the cell does not close the gap. The offline method is also a 20 mm grid, but it takes ray entry against the convex hulls of building A and the building B plate rather than their true footprints. A hull is larger, so the ray meets it sooner and the required RL comes out lower — the observed pattern. Reimplementing it reproduces all 21 values to 0.028 mm. Nothing reported turns on it — every value on both sides is metres clear of the 46.0–55.75 m family.
